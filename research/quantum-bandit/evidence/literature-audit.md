## Verdict and novelty matrix

| Proposed component | Closest primary evidence | Defensible assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Single-arm depth tradeoff q_D(r,δ)=softO(1/r+1/(D r²)) | Erle–Koczor 2608.24434v1, Theorem 1 | Already implied by the August 2026 estimator. Do not claim a new AE primitive. |
| Weak-oracle MAB regret softO(Σ_{Δ_i>0}[1+1/(DΔ_i)]) | Wan-style weak reward oracles plus depth-tunable estimation | Plausible elimination corollary; no directly matching finite-reset regret theorem found in the inspected sources. Novelty is bounded to the precise restricted model and proved reduction. |
| Gap-free upper softO(min{T,K+sqrt(KT/D)}) | Same elimination reduction | Plausible upper bound, with essential hidden horizon/confidence logs. Do not call minimax optimal until a reset-model lower bound is proved. |
| Horizon-independent O(K) endpoint | Liu–Li–Lui 2608.14319v1, Theorem 8 | False uniformly over general weak-oracle instances: Ω(K log(T/K)). softO(K) can hide this logarithm; O(K) cannot. |
| Fixed-bias multi-fidelity ε-BAI intervals | Poiani et al. 2406.03033v2, §2, Theorems 3.1/4.1 | Known mean-bias abstraction and cost objective. Merely adding bias to a confidence radius is not a broad novelty claim. |
| Circuit-derived bias certificate plus quantum depth/cost choices | Combination of circuit stability and multi-fidelity BAI | Potentially distinct contribution, conditional on an explicit certificate theorem, matching inverse access, actual resource costs, adaptive confidence proof, and comparison with existing MF-BAI. |
| Hardware noise or memory-decoherence speedup in general | Channel-oracle and NeurIPS evidence | Not justified by the ideal fixed-unitary/reset theorem. These models must stay separate. |

## Pinned primary-source ledger

**L1. Liu, Li, Lui, Quantum Multi-Armed Bandits and Linear Bandits: Lower Bounds and Algorithms, 2608.14319v1 (14 August 2026).** Full text: [§2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14319v1#S2), [Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14319v1#Thmtheorem8), [Remark 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14319v1#Thmtheorem7). The learner selects an arm classically and queries its reward unitary or inverse at most once per round; both consume horizon and gap regret. Quantum memory is otherwise unrestricted. Controlled unknown-unitary access is not granted; oracle-independent controlled reflections can use ordinary U/U†. The minimax theorem gives Ω(K log(T/K)) for K≥2, T≥CK, including canonical Bernoulli oracles. Its hard family is point-versus-interval testing; a fixed pair of unitary alternatives may be perfectly distinguishable with finitely many coherent queries. Therefore a naïve two-point lower bound cannot establish the horizon logarithm. This stronger-memory policy class contains the finite-reset policies, so its regret lower bound applies to the restricted model as well. Local snapshot: `primary/2608.14319v1.html`; web full-text lines 145–167, 186–191, 199–216.

**L2. Erle, Koczor, Nearly Optimal Amplitude Estimation at any Depth, 2608.24434v1 (25 August 2026).** [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24434v1#Thmtheorem1), [Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24434v1#alg1), measurement model, Definition 1, Corollary 1, Appendix B. A depth-|m| circuit makes |m| total U/U† queries, including preparation and the final state projection. Independent shots use depths sampled from an admissible window, with max |m|≤M. For angle accuracy r≤1/M and failure δ, M²N=O(r⁻² log(r⁻¹δ⁻¹)), uniformly including endpoints. The theorem needs an exact fixed unitary and its inverse, projector/reflections, independent fresh preparations, and classical least-squares processing. It is neither a noisy-channel theorem nor a theorem about an arbitrary maximum-likelihood estimator. The displayed average cost is Q=N E|m|, but the implementation also has deterministic cap Q_actual≤MN. No cross-shot quantum memory is needed. Local snapshot: `primary/2608.24434v1.html`; web full-text lines 40–48, 104–124, 142.

**L3. Giurgica-Tiron et al., Low depth algorithms for quantum amplitude estimation, 2012.03348v2 (22 June 2022), Quantum 6, 745.** [Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03348v2#S2.Thmtheorem3), [Theorem 3.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03348v2#S3.Thmtheorem10), [journal](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2022-06-27-745). Power-law AE has its claimed tradeoff and constant success only where Bernstein–von Mises regularity applies; this is not a uniform finite-sample guarantee usable without checking those conditions. QoPrime has a rigorous correctness proof at discrete β=q/k, k≥2, 1≤q≤k−1, with failure ≤2k exp(−2c); arbitrary δ can be chosen by c. Its total-query/depth tradeoff predates the candidate interpolation. The paper also treats a specified depolarizing noise model, not every systematic or adversarial noise process. The later L2 paper supplies the cleaner arbitrary-depth, endpoint-uniform dependency. Local snapshot: `primary/2012.03348v2.html`.

**L4. Buchholz, Kübler, Schölkopf, Multi-Armed Bandits and Quantum Channel Oracles, 2301.08544v4 (19 March 2025), Quantum 9, 1672.** [§2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08544v4#S2.SS2), [Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08544v4#Thmtheorem6), [journal](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-03-25-1672). Theorem 6 lower-bounds BAI by c(δ,η)H(p), H=Σ_{i>1}Δ_i⁻², for means in [η,1−η] and δ<1/2, under a channel that freshly resamples inaccessible reward randomness per query. Superposed arm access alone does not restore the missing reward-randomness coherence. Reusable sampled oracles are a separate model (Theorem 5). This is not a regret lower bound and is not an impossibility theorem for a purified fixed U with reversible access within D-query blocks. However, it blocks an interpretation of candidate A as a theorem for ordinary fresh noisy samples merely because the arm register is quantum. Local snapshot: `primary/2301.08544v4.html`, extracted exact theorem blocks in its `.blocks.json`.

**L5. Poiani, Degenne, Kaufmann, Metelli, Restelli, Optimal Multi-Fidelity Best-Arm Identification, 2406.03033v2 (26 May 2025).** [§2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03033v2#S2), [Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03033v2#S3.Thmtheorem1), [Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03033v2#S4.Thmtheorem1). Each arm/fidelity has a fixed observation distribution, known cost λ_m, and known bias |μ_{a,m}−μ_{a,M}|≤ξ_m, with ξ_M=0. The highest-fidelity best arm is the target. The model is a canonical exponential family with unique optimum. It proves a KL/cost allocation lower bound and an asymptotically matching gradient algorithm as δ→0. Thus fixed systematic bias and optimal fidelity allocation are established classical ideas. Quantum AE outcomes are not IID samples from those arm distributions, so its KL optimality result cannot simply be transplanted by replacing sample counts with q_D. An ε-PAC theorem with circuit certificates is also a different theorem from its exact BAI statement. Local snapshot: `primary/2406.03033v2.html`.

## Original deduction A: what follows, and what does not

Fix an integer D≥1; reset all quantum registers after each shot/block but retain classical measurements, confidence records, and adaptive arm choices. “No cross-block memory” must specify **no quantum memory**. Removing classical memory as well does not support elimination learning. Charge every U and U† call to the selected arm's gap and to horizon T. Non-oracle preparation, measurement, computation, and classical memory are free only in the stated query model; a wall-clock/gate-cost claim needs their costs separately.

For r∈(0,1], choose M=min(D,floor(1/r)) and an admissible uniform depth window. From L2, take N=ceil(C r⁻²M⁻² log(r⁻¹δ⁻¹)). Since sin² is globally 1-Lipschitz, angle error r suffices for mean error r. Since M≤1/r, the rounding contribution M is dominated by 1/(Mr²). Hence the deterministic query cap is

q_D(r,δ) ≤ C′(1/r+1/(D r²)) log(r⁻¹δ⁻¹).

For general bounded reward encodings, an additional reward-controlled flag rotation reduces the mean to a projector probability; state explicitly that this rotation and its inverse are available and that one resulting state-preparation call contains one charged arm-oracle call. This reduction is not implied by a bare scalar estimator interface.

In dyadic elimination, good estimates preserve an optimal arm and remove arm i once r<cΔ_i. Summing phase costs then gives Σ_i q_D(cΔ_i,δ_phase), and multiplying by Δ_i suggests Σ_{Δ_i>0}(1+1/(DΔ_i)) times logs. For a horizon-safe implementation, incomplete final estimation must be truncated, and the active-arm gap at its start controls that partial phase's regret. Allocate summable conditional failure budgets to the adaptively selected arm/phase calls; choosing total failure O(1/T) gives expected failure regret O(1). A per-call marginal guarantee with unproved independence from adaptive selection is insufficient.

Splitting gaps at η gives Tη plus softO(K+K/(Dη)); optimizing η yields the proposed softO(min(T,K+sqrt(KT/D))). This is a deduction outline, not a completed regret proof. At D=1 it recovers the classical rate; at effective D≥1/r it recovers coherent AE. Extra logs cannot be deleted at the latter endpoint because L1 supplies a horizon-logarithmic lower bound.

The essential unresolved lower leaf is a distinguishability bound for arbitrary adaptive classical transcripts of **reset** quantum blocks with a charged arm-query budget, sufficient to prove Ω(min(T,sqrt(KT/D))) (plus weak-arm search costs). One wants per-block information controlled by Δ² q_block² and Σ q_block²≤DΣq_block, but a Fisher-information calculation alone is not a minimax proof. Classical KL chain rules can fail for quantum states with disjoint supports. L1's lower bound transfers to this subset; AE lower bounds do not by themselves prove the reset-bandit regret lower bound.

## Original deduction B: certificate assumptions and counterexamples

The sound fixed-bias model is: each fidelity ℓ exposes one fixed unitary U~_{iℓ}, its **matching exact adjoint**, an ideal target μ_i, and a proved certificate |μ~_{iℓ}−μ_i|≤B_{iℓ}. AE estimates μ~; its statistical radius r plus B is a valid target radius. A deterministic operator-norm bound ||U~−U||≤η gives, for a fixed projector and initial state, the elementary conservative bias certificate B≤min(1,2η). Gate-by-gate coherent operator errors can be summed by telescoping. Sharper state/channel bounds require the precise norm convention. A certificate involving probabilistic calibration needs its failure budget and acquisition cost included.

Two distinct failure modes cannot be silently covered. (1) Fresh noisy channels or a mismatched inverse do not necessarily represent one stationary approximate U/U†; their depth-dependent distribution errors require a separate robustness theorem. (2) Fixed B does not vanish through repetition. For example, all observed fidelities may have μ~_1=μ~_2=1/2 and B=b, while the two possible ideal instances swap μ_1=1/2+b and μ_2=1/2−b. Their available oracles are identical, and their ideal best arms are opposite with gap 2b. If ε<2b and δ<1/2, no algorithm can be uniformly correct. This gives an explicit identifiability obstruction when certificates never reach a sufficiently fine scale.

A simple sufficient ε-BAI procedure chooses a fidelity with B≤ε/4, estimates every arm to r≤ε/4, and selects the largest estimate. On simultaneous coverage, its ideal loss is ≤2(B+r)≤ε. Its cost includes each arm's c_ℓ q_{Dℓ}(r,δ/K), plus certification/preparation/readout costs if claimed. A refined algorithm can intersect certified intervals from multiple fidelities and eliminate only if its upper endpoint is below another arm's lower endpoint; proving stopping, gap-dependent allocation, and comparison against the cheapest valid fidelity remains necessary. A sum of per-arm independently minimized fidelity costs is not automatically the information-theoretic optimum of L5.

## Open leaves and allowed claim boundary

1. Full formal models of NeurIPS L6/L7; currently official-abstract evidence only.
2. Finite-reset regret proof with deterministic horizon accounting, inverse charges, arbitrary reward encoding, and adaptive conditional coverage.
3. Reset-model lower bound before using “minimax optimal,” especially inverse queries, mixed/ancilla states, and adaptive classical transcripts.
4. Circuit-to-mean certificate with fixed implementation/inverse compatibility, error norms, cost and confidence budget.
5. ε-BAI termination/complexity and an appropriate multi-fidelity lower bound; classical MF-GRAD asymptotic optimality does not certify the proposed quantum algorithm.
6. Novelty should be phrased as a particular composition and resource-model theorem. Literature support, local Lean correctness, and an implemented estimator are three different evidence levels.
