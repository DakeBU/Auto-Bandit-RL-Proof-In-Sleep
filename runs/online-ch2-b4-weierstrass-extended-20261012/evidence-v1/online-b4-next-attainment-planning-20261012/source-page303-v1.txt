Definition A.2 A topological space X is a Hausdorff space if any two distinct points can
be separated by disjoint open sets. That is, for any x, y ∈ X with x ̸= y , there exist open
sets U, V ⊆ X such that x ∈ U , y ∈ V , and U ∩ V = ∅.
  Note that Euclidean spaces are Hausdorff spaces.
Theorem A.3 (Weierstrass theorem for extended-real-valued functions (Bauschke et al.,
2003, Theorem 1.29)) Let X be a Hausdorff space, let f : X → [−∞, +∞] be lower
semicontinuous, and let V be a compact subset of X . Suppose V ∩ dom f ̸= ∅. Then, f
achieves its infimum over V .
