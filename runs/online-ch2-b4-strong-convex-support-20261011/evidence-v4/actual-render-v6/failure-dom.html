    Convexity with extended-real values

    Real-height epigraphs, effective domains, constraint indicators and the source convexity characterization.

    Orientation

    Who should read this. Readers of Orabona Chapter 2 convex-analysis foundations.

    Learning goals

    • Keep positive and negative infinity distinct from finite real values.
    • Understand why an epigraph characterizes convexity.
    • Convert between epigraph convexity and interior-weight inequalities under the exact source assumptions.
    • Distinguish finite constrained loss from the below-top domain and retain no-bottom on the domain identity.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    Real heights keep extended-real convexity precise

    These are the library proof routes for the selected definitions, domain and indicator consequences, Theorem 2.4 and Examples 2.5–2.6. Orabona cites Rockafellar for Theorem 2.4 and leaves the norm example as an exercise.

    1. 01
      Project the epigraph to the effective domain

      Every value below positive infinity has a real upper bound, even when the value is negative infinity. Lift two domain points to real epigraph heights. Convexity combines these lifts; the resulting finite height witnesses domain membership.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Domain points admit real epigraph heights; their convex combinations do too.domf={x:f(x)<+∞},epiRf={(x,r):f(x)≤r, r∈R}.Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Use real values only where finiteness is proved

      For Theorem 2.4, the no-negative-infinity hypothesis and domain membership prove that each value is finite. Convexity of the effective domain proves this also for the interpolated point. The real epigraph equivalence then translates the ordinary convexity inequality in both directions. Infinite values are never globally identified with zero.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Under no-bottom and a convex effective domain, the strict-weight inequality is equivalent to epigraph convexity.f(θx+(1−θ)y)≤θf(x)+(1−θ)f(y),x,y∈domf,0<θ<1.Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Read constraints as epigraph intersections

      The indicator epigraph is the product of the feasible set and nonnegative real heights. Adding an indicator intersects the original epigraph with the feasible cylinder; the no-bottom assumption excludes ambiguous infinite addition. Convexity follows from products and intersections of convex sets.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Indicator convexity is equivalent to convexity of V; adding it restricts the epigraph to V.epiRιV=V×[0,∞),epiR(f+ιV)=epiRf∩(V×R).Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Check the affine and norm examples

      Affine evaluation preserves convex combinations exactly. For the norm, apply the triangle inequality followed by nonnegative homogeneity. The real-valued coercion bridge transfers these inequalities into the same extended-real epigraph definition.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Affine functions satisfy equality; the norm satisfies the convexity inequality.‖θx+(1−θ)y‖≤θ‖x‖+(1−θ)‖y‖,0≤θ≤1.Swipe to read the full formula →
    5. 05
      Separate finite loss from the below-top domain

      Inside V the indicator is zero. Outside V it is top: adding bottom gives bottom, and adding a finite value or top gives top, never a finite real. Thus a finite constrained value requires both feasibility and a finite original value. The separate domain identity excludes bottom globally, so outside V the sum is top.

      Step 5 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Finite values exclude both infinities; the below-top domain excludes only positive infinity.f(x)+ιV(x)∈R⟺x∈V ∧ f(x)∈R;f>−∞⇒dom(f+ιV)=domf∩V.Swipe to read the full formula →

    Reading boundary. Real modules and normed spaces generalize the Euclidean examples; the pointwise finite-loss algebra permits arbitrary carriers. Empty domains remain allowed. No-bottom is essential for the separate domain-intersection result, not for the finite-loss iff. Closure constructions, optimality and Jensen have separate bounded readers; Chapter 2 remains incomplete. The source passage does not specify mixed-infinity arithmetic or a total ambient extension of the original game losses; the pointwise result uses an explicitly chosen ordinary EReal interpretation.
