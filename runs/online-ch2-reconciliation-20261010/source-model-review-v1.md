A classical Option selector chooses a witness exactly when one exists. First terminal: select(L)=some p implies M(L,p), and select(L)=none iff no p satisfies M(L,p). Any stronger some-p equivalence must avoid falsely forcing a particular minimizer in a tie.

Then derive strict-past prediction from the list of loss indices range t, prefix invariance under equality of all past restricted losses, and the initial-round rule using an explicitly supplied x0 in V. For t>0 no attained minimizer gives none; do not use a default action while claiming it minimizes. No convexity, compactness, measurability or executable selection theorem is needed for this relational foundation.
