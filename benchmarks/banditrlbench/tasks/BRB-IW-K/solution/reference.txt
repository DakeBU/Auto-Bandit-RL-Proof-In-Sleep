Given H, the finite law is p, so summing over I gives E[hat(l)_i|H]=p_i(l_i/p_i)=l_i and E[hat(l)_i^2|H]=p_i(l_i/p_i)^2=l_i^2/p_i. Positive support allows division and observation of arm i; at p_i=0 a nonzero loss is never observed and no zero-filled estimator can recover it. Using denominator q_i while sampling with p gives p_i l_i/q_i, generally biased. Fixing losses given H prevents the loss coordinate from depending on the freshly sampled action. For two fair arms and a fixed named i, let the adversary set L_i=1{I=i} after observing I: E[2 L_i 1{I=i}|H]=1 while E[L_i|H]=1/2. Predictability (or a suitable conditional independence/law condition) excludes this dependence. Across-round independence is unnecessary for the conditional one-round calculation. A regret rate also requires an algorithmic potential argument and control of accumulated estimation errors.
