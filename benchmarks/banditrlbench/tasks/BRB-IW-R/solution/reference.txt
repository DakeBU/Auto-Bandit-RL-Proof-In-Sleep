Only I=i contributes to either raw moment. Thus E[hat(l)_i]=l_i and E[hat(l)_i^2]=l_i^2/p_i. Expanding the square and using sum_j p_j=1 yields E[(hat(l)_i-l_i)^2]=l_i^2/p_i-2l_i^2+l_i^2=l_i^2(1/p_i-1). The bound confuses bounded losses with inverse-probability-weighted estimates: a nonzero estimate is l_i/p_i, potentially greater than one. Let p=(1/4,3/4), l=(1,0), and i=1. The estimate is 4 with probability 1/4 and 0 with probability 3/4, has mean 1 and variance (1/4)9+(3/4)1=3>1/4. If p_i=0 and l_i=1, the arm is never sampled and a zero-filled estimate has mean zero, not one. Defining division by zero does not restore information or unbiasedness. A different positive p_i with a fully computed violation is equally valid.
