# CUCB source model, deterministic triggering and impossible case

2026-09-18. Partial progress under contract `81a1998`; full performance endpoints and independent acceptance remain open, accepted topics 0/10.

`Algorithms/CUCBDeterministicTrigger` first proves a reusable actual-trajectory support theorem: a measurable round property holding almost surely under every input's actual round kernel holds almost surely at every coordinate of the infinite CUCB trajectory. It uses the genuine initial coordinate law, prefix/successor joint law and countable conjunction. The deterministic trigger proof then derives observation probability one from the primitive lower bound p_i=1, integrates through the actual oracle/environment mixture, and obtains N_i(n)<=T_i(n) almost surely for every n. It does not assert support on null feedback records, nor replace this exact source branch by a positive failure bound.

`Algorithms/CUCBFeedbackModel` assembles finite feasible superarms (an injective selected-arm-set map), nonempty possible-trigger sets, selected-arm inclusion and probability-one observation, triggerability of every retained arm, fixed bounded marginal laws and primitive ObservationCompatible, plus nonnegative integrable aggregate reward. Cross-arm dependence is allowed and realized reward has no uniform bound. `minTrigger` is the finite minimum of the actual environment observation probabilities over actions that can trigger the arm. Its positivity, <=1 bound and actionwise lower-bound property are proved. The exact nice event, probabilistic charged tail and deterministic branch instantiate with these computed parameters, not an unrelated supplied probability.

`Algorithms/CUCBSourceModel` adds the complete frozen primitive score/oracle specification: nonnegative scores agreeing with actual expected reward at the true input, coordinate monotonicity, bounded smoothness on possible-trigger arms, continuous strictly increasing nonnegative-domain modulus with value zero at zero, alpha/beta in (0,1], measurable randomized oracle with per-input alpha-success probability >=beta, and the disclosed positive-gap inverse range clause. The optimum is the maximum over the actual finite feasible family. Badness, signed action gap and inverse-gap parameter are derived, as is the ChargeData used by the actual normalized counter recurrence. Inverse positivity and exact modulus equality, optimum monotonicity, normalized threshold sufficiency and no-bad-action zero gap/count boundaries are proved.

This is the complete primitive model specification and parameter connection, not yet a finished performance theorem or a concrete noisy witness. The oracle's trajectory conditional-success theorem and actual/mean reward expectation identity still have to be proved from this structure.

`Algorithms/CUCBImpossibleCase` proves the source constant-six radius implication and the actual-index impossible case. On the exact empirical-mean nice event, an alpha-successful action cannot have positive true gap if every potentially triggered arm has T_i>6 log(t)/(f^{-1}(gap))^2. The proof uses the actual clipped CUCB index, its established optimism, a finite maximum over possible-trigger errors and strict monotonicity of the modulus. Strict threshold comparisons, zero observations and t=n+1 are preserved.

`Algorithms/CUCBGapInverse` extends the inverse from individual action gaps to the entire frozen interval (0,Delta_max]. It proves positivity, exact inversion, uniqueness, strict monotonicity and agreement with inverseGap at actual bad-action gaps. The exact piecewise sampling threshold is antitone in the gap and interval-integrable between any two positive source-domain endpoints (including equal/reversed endpoints). The zero default outside the domain is explicit and is not used to claim inversion there. This closes the scalar inverse/integrability prerequisite; it does not prove the refined regret integral bound itself.

`Tests/CUCBSourceModelCanary` records dependency audits of these declarations. It is a dependency check, not the required concrete noisy final-performance canary. No independent semantic review or controlled experiment has been reported as complete.
