    6. Thompson sampling and Bayesian regret

    The scoped stationary finite-arm Thompson route compiles from posterior kernels and probability matching on the actual recursive generated history through clipped-UCB decomposition, latent-stream confidence, and an explicit Bayesian regret terminal.

    Thompson sampling

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Condition

      Form the posterior distribution of the unknown environment given observed history.

    2. 02
      Sample a model

      Draw one plausible environment from that posterior.

    3. 03
      Optimize the sample

      Choose an arm that is best in the sampled environment.

    4. 04
      Observe and update

      Record the reward and condition the posterior on the enlarged history.

    Algorithm · explicit conventions

    Posterior-sampling loop

    This form makes probability matching visible: sample one environment from the current posterior, then act optimally for that sampled environment.

    Worked example · follow one decision

    Read one Thompson decision as probability matching

    After the current history, suppose the posterior says arm 1 is optimal with probability 0.7 and arm 2 with probability 0.3. Thompson sampling turns that uncertainty directly into its next-action law.

    1. 01
      Start from posterior uncertainty

      The probabilities describe uncertainty about the true optimal arm under the Bayesian model, not empirical-mean scores.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Given the previous history, the posterior probability that arm 1 is optimal is 0.7 and that arm 2 is optimal is 0.3.\[\Pr(A^\star=1\mid H_{t-1})=0.7,\qquad \Pr(A^\star=2\mid H_{t-1})=0.3.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Sample a plausible environment

      If this posterior draw makes arm 2 optimal, the documented selector chooses arm 2 even though arm 1 is more likely to be truly optimal.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: A sampled environment is drawn from the posterior; in this illustrative draw arm 2 is best, so arm 2 is selected.\[\widetilde\theta_t\sim\Pi_t,\qquad \operatorname*{arg\,max}_a\mu_a(\widetilde\theta_t)=2\Longrightarrow A_t=2.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Recover the action probabilities

      Across posterior draws, applying the same optimal-arm rule reproduces the posterior law of the true optimal arm.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Conditioned on the history, Thompson sampling chooses arms 1 and 2 with probabilities 0.7 and 0.3.\[\Pr(A_t=1\mid H_{t-1})=0.7,\qquad \Pr(A_t=2\mid H_{t-1})=0.3.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. Thompson sampling explores because posterior uncertainty remains: a 30 percent chance that arm 2 is optimal becomes a 30 percent chance of sampling it.

    Scope. The numbers specify one hypothetical posterior state and do not perform a Bayesian update. The exact compiled route below constructs the posterior kernel and proves probability matching under its stated latent-environment and tie-breaking contracts.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    Why posterior sampling can be analyzed through probability matching

    The analysis uses the fact that the action selected from a posterior sample and the truly optimal action have the same conditional distribution given the visible history.

    1. 01
      Condition on the observed history

      Given the current history, both the unknown environment and the fresh posterior sample are distributed according to the same posterior kernel.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Conditionally on the previous history, the sampled and true latent environments share the current posterior law.\[\mathcal L(\widetilde\theta_t\mid H_{t-1})=\mathcal L(\theta_\star\mid H_{t-1})=\Pi_t.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Match the optimal-action law

      Applying the same documented optimal-arm selector to those two latent environments gives equal conditional laws for the chosen arm and the optimal arm.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: For each arm, its conditional chance of being selected equals its conditional chance of being optimal.\[\mathbb P(A_t=a\mid H_{t-1})=\mathbb P(A^\star=a\mid H_{t-1}).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Rewrite Bayesian regret

      Probability matching permits a clipped upper-confidence decomposition in which uncertainty and failure terms are separated before expectations are summed.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Conditional Bayesian regret is bounded by an uncertainty term for the selected arm plus an upper-confidence failure term for the optimal arm.\[\mathbb E_t[\mu(A^\star)-\mu(A_t)]\le\mathbb E_t[U_t(A_t)-\mu(A_t)]+\mathbb E_t[\mu(A^\star)-U_t(A^\star)].\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Sum uncertainty under the model contract

      The bounded-reward, variance, and posterior assumptions then control the accumulated widths and failure charge, producing the source theorem's Bayesian rate.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Under the stated Bayesian finite-arm assumptions, the accumulated terms yield a constant times square root of k n log n.\[BR_n\le C\sqrt{kn\log n}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Reading boundary. This is a conceptual map of the Bayesian route. The exact compiled terminal has its own boundedness and variance terms and does not establish a frequentist guarantee for arbitrary Thompson-sampling implementations.

    Source theorem · faithful restatement

    Theorem 36.1 (Bayesian regret of Thompson sampling)

    Original at online p. 461 (Chapter 36, §36.1) ↗

    Probability matching turns posterior uncertainty about the best arm into an exploration policy.

    Model
    A k-armed Bayesian bandit environment with prior Q and arm laws indexed by the latent environment.
    Assumptions
    For every environment and arm, the centered reward law is 1-sub-Gaussian and its mean lies in [0,1]; optimal-arm ties use the algorithm's fixed rule.
    Algorithm parameters
    Prior Q, arm count k, and horizon n.
    Regret notion
    Bayesian regret BR_n, averaged over the prior, observations, and policy randomization.
    Guarantee
    BR_n is at most C√(kn log n) for a universal constant C.
    Source mathematical statement. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: In the stated Bayesian finite-armed setting, Thompson sampling has square-root-horizon Bayesian regret up to a logarithmic factor.\[BR_n\le C\sqrt{kn\log n}\quad\text{for the theorem's Bayesian finite-armed model and universal }C.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    The canonical posterior is the conditional environment law

    Lean declarationBanditRLProof.PosteriorKernel.canonicalPosterior_kernel_ae_eq_condDistrib_of_pair_map_eq

    Compiled

    Plain-English statement. If an environment and observed history have the canonical prior-likelihood joint law, then the canonical posterior kernel equals the conditional law of the environment given that history almost everywhere.

    Mathematical reading. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: If an environment and observed history have the canonical prior-likelihood joint law, then the canonical posterior kernel equals the conditional law of the environment given that history almost everywhere.\(\Pi(\cdot\mid H)=\mathcal L(E\mid H)\quad\text{a.e.}\)Swipe to read the full formula →
    Intuition
    A posterior is a measurable kernel from histories to environments; the theorem identifies that constructed kernel with the measure-theoretic conditional distribution.
    Why it is needed
    Probability matching is valid only after the algorithmic posterior is tied to the actual environment-history law.
    Place in the proof
    It is the posterior-law root of the Chapter 6 proof DAG.
    Proof and Lean reading notes
    Proof idea
    Use uniqueness of regular conditional distributions under the equality of the observed pair pushforward and the canonical prior-likelihood joint measure.
    Lean reading notes

    Compiled

    Clipping keeps scores inside the known mean range while empirical means plus widths retain optimism with high probability.
