    EXP3 with importance-weighted losses

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Turn weights into probabilities

      Normalize positive arm weights into the current sampling law.

    2. 02
      Sample an arm

      Draw the current action from that probability vector.

    3. 03
      Estimate all losses

      Use the observed loss divided by its sampling probability for the selected arm; use zero for the others.

    4. 04
      Update exponentially

      Downweight arms according to their estimated cumulative losses.

    5. 05
      Separate guarantees

      Expected, fixed-horizon tail, all-time, and sparse-loss results use distinct concentration interfaces.

    Algorithm · explicit conventions

    EXP3 importance-weighted update

    The loop names the sampling law, the one-coordinate unbiased loss estimate, and the exponential update used by the potential argument.

    Sampling, estimation, and weight update. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Normalize positive weights, sample an arm, divide the observed loss by that arm's probability, and exponentially downweight each arm by its estimated loss.\[p_t(a)=\frac{w_t(a)}{\sum_b w_t(b)},\qquad \widehat\ell_t(a)=\frac{\ell_t(A_t)\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}}{p_t(a)},\qquad w_{t+1}(a)=w_t(a)e^{-\eta\widehat\ell_t(a)}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    input: k arms, horizon n, learning rate η; initialize w_1(a) = 1
for t = 1,…,n:
    normalize weights into the sampling law p_t
    sample A_t ~ p_t and observe only loss ℓ_t(A_t)
    set ℓ̂_t(a) = ℓ_t(A_t) 1{A_t=a} / p_t(a) for every arm
    update w_{t+1}(a) = w_t(a) exp(-η ℓ̂_t(a))

    Source/Lean boundary. The displayed equations are the textbook core. BanditRLlib's generated predictable route makes positive support and any exploration mixture explicit, and its expected, fixed-window, all-prefix, realized, and sparse-loss guarantees remain separate theorems with separate confidence contracts.

    Worked example · follow one decision

    One EXP3 update from a single observed loss

    Start with two equal weights, learning rate η = 0.25, and suppose the sampled second arm incurs loss 0.8. The update shows why dividing by the sampling probability is both necessary and noisy.

    1. 01
      Sample from equal weights

      Both arms initially have the same probability; this particular history samples arm 2.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Both initial weights are one, both probabilities are one half, and arm 2 is sampled with observed loss 0.8.\[w_1=(1,1),\qquad p_1=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right),\qquad A_1=2,\quad \ell_1(A_1)=0.8.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Reconstruct an unbiased loss coordinate

      Only arm 2 is observed. Dividing by its probability produces a larger estimate whose conditional expectation equals the hidden loss coordinate.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The estimated loss is zero for arm 1 and 1.6 for arm 2 because the observed 0.8 is divided by probability one half.\[\widehat\ell_1(1)=0,\qquad \widehat\ell_1(2)=\frac{0.8}{1/2}=1.6.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Downweight the observed loss

      The exponential update lowers arm 2's weight, so arm 1 becomes more likely at the next round without assigning arm 2 zero probability.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The next weights are approximately 1 and 0.6703, so arm 1 receives probability about 0.599.\[w_2=(1,e^{-0.4})\approx(1,0.6703),\qquad p_2(1)=\frac1{1+e^{-0.4}}\approx0.599.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. Importance weighting repairs the missing feedback in expectation, while its enlarged squared magnitude creates the variance term that the EXP3 proof must pay for.

    Scope. This is one deterministic arithmetic trace conditional on a sampled action. It is not an adversarial-regret experiment and does not merge the expected, fixed-window, all-prefix, or realized-regret theorem contracts.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    How one bandit observation becomes an adversarial regret guarantee

    EXP3 couples an unbiased importance-weighted estimator with the exponential-weights potential. The proof must pay explicitly for the estimator's second moment.

    1. 01
      Recover every coordinate in expectation

      Although only the selected loss is observed, positive sampling probability makes the importance-weighted estimate conditionally unbiased.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Given the past, the importance-weighted estimated loss of each arm has expectation equal to that arm's actual loss.\[\mathbb E_t[\widehat\ell_t(a)]=\ell_t(a).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Compare exponential potentials

      The log change of total weight compares the algorithm's estimated loss with any fixed arm, leaving a learning-rate times second-moment penalty.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Estimated regret is at most log k over eta plus eta over two times the accumulated estimated-loss second moment.\[\sum_t\langle p_t,\widehat\ell_t\rangle-\sum_t\widehat\ell_t(a)\le\frac{\log k}{\eta}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_t\langle p_t,\widehat\ell_t^2\rangle.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Pay for bandit noise

      Importance weighting inflates individual observations, but averaging under the sampling law bounds the conditional second-moment contribution by the number of arms.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The conditional expected weighted squared estimate is at most the number of arms.\[\mathbb E_t\langle p_t,\widehat\ell_t^2\rangle\le k.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Balance the two costs

      Choosing the learning rate so that the initial-complexity and variance terms match yields the square-root expected-regret rate.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: With the balanced learning rate, expected regret is at most the square root of two n k log k.\[\eta=\sqrt{\frac{2\log k}{nk}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_n\le\sqrt{2nk\log k}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Reading boundary. The bridge follows the textbook expected-regret argument. BanditRLlib's fixed-window, all-prefix, realized, and sparse-loss extensions have additional hypotheses and are intentionally not collapsed into this one card.

    Source theorem · faithful restatement

    Theorem 11.2 (EXP3 expected regret)

    Original at online p. 156 ↗

    Importance weighting makes the bandit observation unbiased, while exponential weights controls the resulting estimated-loss regret.

    Model
    An adversarial k-armed bandit with a fixed reward table x in [0,1]^(n×k).
    Assumptions
    The policy is the textbook EXP3 construction and compares with the best fixed arm in hindsight.
    Algorithm parameters
    Horizon n, arm count k, and learning rate η = √(2 log k/(nk)).
    Regret notion
    Expected adversarial pseudo-regret R_n against the best fixed arm.
    Guarantee
    R_n ≤ √(2nk log k).
    Source mathematical statement. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: With the textbook learning rate, EXP3 has expected adversarial regret at most the square root of two times horizon, arm count, and log arm count.\[\eta=\sqrt{\frac{2\log k}{nk}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_n\le\sqrt{2nk\log k}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Place in the proof

    Lean reading notes
