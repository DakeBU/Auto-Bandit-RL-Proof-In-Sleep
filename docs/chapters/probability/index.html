    A reusable confidence-event route

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Model conditional noise

      State a conditional MGF or bounded-reward assumption on the generated history.

    2. 02
      Derive one tail bound

      Convert the MGF bound into a deviation probability at a fixed index.

    3. 03
      Allocate confidence

      Assign a finite, geometric, or telescoping share δ_i to every arm and time.

    4. 04
      Join the events

      Use a union bound to obtain one good event consumed by an algorithm proof.

    Worked example · follow one decision

    Spend one confidence budget across six events

    Suppose two arms are inspected at three checkpoints and the total allowed failure probability is delta = 0.05. The union-bound step is visible without hiding the accounting inside an algorithm.

    1. 01
      Name the six bad events

      Each event says that one arm's empirical estimate misses its confidence radius at one checkpoint.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: There is one deviation event for each of two arms at each of three checkpoints, for six events in total.\[E_{a,j}=\{\lvert\widehat\mu_{a,j}-\mu_a\rvert>r_{a,j}\},\qquad a\in\{1,2\},\ j\in\{1,2,3\}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Give every event an explicit share

      An equal schedule assigns delta divided by six to each fixed-index tail bound.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Each event receives failure budget one over 120, and its fixed-index tail probability is bounded by that share.\[\delta_{a,j}=\frac{0.05}{6}=\frac1{120},\qquad \Pr(E_{a,j})\le\frac1{120}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Join the events once

      No independence is needed for the union bound; adding the six shares preserves the declared total budget.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The chance that any of the six deviations occurs is at most the sum of their probabilities, which is 0.05.\[\Pr\!\left(\bigcup_{a=1}^{2}\bigcup_{j=1}^{3}E_{a,j}\right)\le\sum_{a,j}\Pr(E_{a,j})\le6\cdot\frac1{120}=0.05.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. A simultaneous good event is a budgeted collection of fixed-index facts. Finite, geometric, and telescoping schedules differ in how they distribute delta, not in the final union-bound logic.

    Scope. The event probabilities are illustrative inputs to the union bound. The compiled concentration declarations below specify the MGF, measurability, filtration, and index assumptions that justify each tail estimate.

    Source theorem · faithful restatement

    Corollary 5.5 (sub-Gaussian tail)

    Original at online pp. 77–78 ↗

    The fixed-index tail estimate is the mathematical atom from which finite and all-time confidence schedules are assembled.

    Model
    A real-valued random variable X on a probability space.
    Assumptions
    X is centered and σ-sub-Gaussian in the moment-generating-function sense; the deviation ε is nonnegative.
    Algorithm parameters
    Sub-Gaussian scale σ and deviation threshold ε.
    Regret notion
    Not a regret theorem; this is a one-sided concentration primitive consumed by later regret proofs.
    Guarantee
    The upper-tail probability is at most exp(-ε²/(2σ²)).
    Source mathematical statement. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: A centered sub-Gaussian variable has an exponentially small upper-tail probability.\[\Pr(X\ge\varepsilon)\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\quad\text{for a zero-mean }\sigma^2\text{-sub-Gaussian }X.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
