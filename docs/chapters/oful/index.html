    5. OFUL, self-normalized confidence, and stopping times

    The scoped canonical finite-action linear-bandit route compiles from elliptical potential and self-normalized ridge confidence through one horizon-free generated OFUL policy with all-horizon regret and stopping consumers, plus a separately identified horizon-indexed expected-consistency family.

    algorithm · ordered flow

    OFUL confidence-ellipsoid loop

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Update geometry

      Accumulate the regularized feature Gram matrix from past actions.

    2. 02
      Estimate

      Compute the ridge least-squares parameter estimate.

    3. 03
      Build confidence

      Place plausible parameters in an ellipsoid around the estimate.

    4. 04
      Act optimistically

      Choose the action and plausible parameter pair with the largest predicted reward.

    5. 05
      Charge widths

      Bound regret by confidence widths and use the elliptical-potential sum.

    Algorithm · explicit conventions

    OFUL optimistic ridge loop

    The compact loop separates the state carried between rounds from the confidence maximization performed at the current round.

    Ridge estimate and optimistic score. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Form the regularized Gram matrix and ridge estimate, then choose the feasible action with the largest estimated reward plus its confidence width.\[V_t=\lambda I+\sum_{s<t}x_sx_s^\top,\quad \widehat\theta_t=V_t^{-1}\sum_{s<t}x_sy_s,\quad x_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in\mathcal D_t}\left(\langle\widehat\theta_t,x\rangle+\beta_t\lVert x\rVert_{V_t^{-1}}\right).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    input: regularization λ > 0, confidence δ, feasible action sets D_t
initialize V_1 = λ I and b_1 = 0
for t = 1,…,n:
    set θ̂_t = V_t^{-1} b_t and compute confidence radius β_t
    choose a documented maximizer of <θ̂_t,x> + β_t ||x||_{V_t^{-1}}
    observe y_t; update V_{t+1} = V_t + x_t x_t^T and b_{t+1} = b_t + x_t y_t

    Worked example · follow one decision

    Why an unexplored direction can beat a better estimate

    Use two-dimensional features with actions e₁ and e₂, regularization λ = 1, and confidence radius β₂ = 0.5. After one modest reward from e₁, OFUL compares estimated reward and directional uncertainty.

    1. 01
      Record one observed direction

      The first action is e₁ and its reward is 0.2, so only the first coordinate enters the data vector.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The policy observes reward 0.2 after choosing the first basis vector, so the data vector is (0.2, 0).\[x_1=e_1,\qquad y_1=0.2,\qquad b_2=x_1y_1=(0.2,0)^\top.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      See uncertainty in the Gram geometry

      The observed direction receives an extra rank-one update; the untouched direction retains the larger inverse-Gram width.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The Gram matrix is diagonal with entries 2 and 1; the estimate is (0.1, 0), and the unexplored second direction has the wider confidence norm.\[V_2=I+e_1e_1^\top=\operatorname{diag}(2,1),\quad \widehat\theta_2=V_2^{-1}b_2=(0.1,0)^\top,\quad \lVert e_1\rVert_{V_2^{-1}}=\frac1{\sqrt2}<\lVert e_2\rVert_{V_2^{-1}}=1.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Let optimism choose information

      The small estimated advantage of e₁ is outweighed by e₂'s wider confidence interval, so the optimistic score explores e₂.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The optimistic scores are about 0.454 for the first direction and 0.5 for the unexplored second direction, so OFUL chooses the second direction.\[U_2(e_1)=0.1+\frac{0.5}{\sqrt2}\approx0.454,\qquad U_2(e_2)=0+0.5=0.5,\qquad x_2=e_2.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. OFUL explores directions, not named arms in isolation. The same inverse-Gram norm that makes e₂ attractive here is later summed by the elliptical-potential argument.

    Scope. This two-dimensional calculation illustrates one optimistic score comparison. It is not a trajectory benchmark and does not instantiate every boundedness, measurability, or confidence-radius hypothesis in the compiled theorem.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    How confidence geometry pays for linear-bandit regret

    OFUL replaces one confidence interval per arm by one ellipsoid for the unknown parameter. The same inverse-Gram norm measures both uncertainty and the cost charged to the trajectory.

    1. 01
      Put the parameter in an ellipsoid

      A self-normalized concentration result bounds the ridge-estimation error in the geometry accumulated from earlier features.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The true parameter lies within beta t of the ridge estimate in the Gram-matrix norm.\[\lVert\widehat\theta_t-\theta_\star\rVert_{V_t}\le\beta_t.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Convert optimism into one width

      Maximizing over the confidence set makes the instantaneous regret no larger than a constant multiple of the selected feature's inverse-Gram width.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: One-step regret is controlled by twice the confidence radius times the selected feature's inverse-Gram norm.\[r_t\le 2\beta_t\lVert x_t\rVert_{V_t^{-1}}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Sum widths through determinant growth

      The elliptical-potential lemma says that repeatedly visiting a direction increases the Gram determinant and prevents that direction from remaining wide forever.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The sum of clipped squared confidence widths is at most twice the logarithmic growth of the Gram determinant.\[\sum_{t=1}^{n}\min\{1,\lVert x_t\rVert_{V_t^{-1}}^2\}\le 2\log\frac{\det V_{n+1}}{\det V_1}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Finish with Cauchy-Schwarz

      Combining the confidence radius with the potential bound yields the familiar dimension-times-square-root-horizon scale, with the source assumptions determining the logarithms and constants.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Cumulative regret is controlled by the final confidence radius times the square root of horizon and determinant growth, giving dimension times square-root horizon up to logs.\[R_n\lesssim\beta_n\sqrt{n\log\frac{\det V_{n+1}}{\det V_1}}=\widetilde O(d\sqrt n).\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Reading boundary. This bridge identifies the source proof joints and their local counterparts; it is not a claim that the finite-action Lean route reproduces every OFUL variant or the paper's sharp constants verbatim.
