    8. Tsallis-FTRL, corruption, and nonstationarity

    The scoped canonical half-Tsallis FTRL route compiles from finite-simplex minimizers and one-step stability through a measurable scheduled generated trajectory, score alignment, expected self-bounding, and a finite-arm IID bounded reward-law logarithmic regret terminal; corruption and nonstationary routes remain labelled extensions.

    Half-Tsallis FTRL update

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Estimate bandit losses

      Construct an importance-weighted or reduced-variance estimate from the selected arm.

    2. 02
      Accumulate the estimate

      Add the new estimate to the running loss vector.

    3. 03
      Regularize with half-Tsallis entropy

      Choose the next distribution by minimizing estimated loss plus the scheduled Tsallis potential.

    4. 04
      Sample and adapt

      Draw the next arm and update the learning-rate schedule; specialized theorems then add self-bounding, corruption, drift, or restart assumptions.

    Worked example · follow one decision

    See estimator noise and gap credit in one round

    At one two-arm round, suppose the FTRL distribution is p = (0.25, 0.75), arm 1 is sampled, and its observed loss is 0.5. The arithmetic exposes the two quantities that the regularizer must reconcile.

    1. 01
      Importance-weight the observed coordinate

      Only arm 1 is observed, so its loss is divided by the probability with which it was sampled.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Arm 1 is sampled with probability 0.25 and loss 0.5, producing estimated loss vector (2, 0).\[A_t=1,\quad \ell_t(A_t)=0.5,\qquad \widehat\ell_t=(0.5/0.25,0)=(2,0).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Notice the rare-arm variance cost

      A small sampling probability makes the realized estimate large; its conditional second moment carries the inverse-probability geometry.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The conditional second moment of arm 1's importance-weighted estimate is 0.25 divided by 0.25, which equals 1.\[\mathbb E_t[\widehat\ell_t(1)^2]=\frac{0.5^2}{0.25}=1.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Compare the stochastic gap credit

      If arm 2 is optimal and arm 1 has mean-loss gap 0.2, the same probability mass contributes a smaller but structured self-bounding term.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: With gap 0.2, sampling arm 1 with probability 0.25 contributes instantaneous expected gap cost 0.05.\[\Delta_1p_t(1)=0.2\cdot0.25=0.05.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. The estimator creates an inverse-probability noise charge, while stochastic gaps price suboptimal probability mass. Half-Tsallis curvature is useful because its stability geometry can connect these two scales.

    Scope. This is a one-round calculation, not a derivation of Tsallis-INF or a compiled general curvature-noise-gap calculus. The theorem cards below remain authoritative for schedules, estimators, constants, and regimes.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    Where curvature, estimator noise, and stochastic gaps meet

    Tsallis-INF is useful as a reading lens because its guarantee is assembled from three interacting ingredients rather than from the regularizer alone.

    1. 01
      Let curvature control stability

      Schematically, the FTRL regularizer determines how much the chosen distribution can move when one more estimated loss vector is added.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: One-step FTRL stability is controlled by the regularizer's local inverse curvature.\[\operatorname{stab}_t\lesssim\left\langle\widehat\ell_t,(\nabla^2\Psi_t)^{-1}\widehat\ell_t\right\rangle.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Match curvature to bandit noise

      The estimator contributes probability-dependent variance, so the useful curvature is the one that absorbs the same rare-arm geometry.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The estimated loss second moment grows like the squared loss divided by the arm's sampling probability.\[\mathbb E_t[\widehat\ell_t(a)^2]\asymp\frac{\ell_t(a)^2}{p_t(a)}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Add stochastic self-bounding when available

      In an IID gap model, expected regret also lower-bounds the probability mass assigned to suboptimal arms; this can repay part of the stability cost.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Stochastic regret equals the sum over rounds and suboptimal arms of gap times expected sampling probability.\[Reg_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a\ne a^\star}\Delta_a\,\mathbb E[p_t(a)].\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Choose the regime-specific consumer

      The adversarial analysis keeps the stability charge, while stochastic, corrupted, drifting, and restart results add distinct assumptions to sharpen or reorganize it.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Curvature, estimator noise, and a stated self-bounding or drift contract combine only within a particular regret theorem.\[\begin{gathered}\mathcal C+\mathcal V-\mathcal G\Longrightarrow Reg_T\text{ bound},\\ \mathcal C=\text{curvature},\quad\mathcal V=\text{noise},\quad\mathcal G=\text{gap credit}.\end{gathered}\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Source theorem · faithful restatement

    Zimmert–Seldin, Theorem 1 (adversarial regime)

    Original at paper p. 9 ↗

    The half-Tsallis regularizer is designed to retain adversarial robustness while adapting sharply to stochastic self-bounding structure.

    Model
    Any adversarial K-armed bandit problem in the paper's loss-based protocol.
    Assumptions
    Tsallis-INF uses α = 1/2, symmetric regularization, and either the importance-weighted or reduced-variance estimator specified in the paper.
    Algorithm parameters
    Horizon T, arm count K, and schedule η_t = 2/√t for IW or 4/√t for RV.
    Regret notion
    Adversarial pseudo-regret Reg_T.
    Guarantee
    The IW route gives 4√(KT)+1; the RV route gives 2√(KT)+10K log T+16.
    Source mathematical statement. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The source theorem gives explicit adversarial square-root regret bounds for importance-weighted and reduced-variance Tsallis-INF variants.\[Reg_T\le4\sqrt{KT}+1\quad\text{(importance weighting)},\qquad Reg_T\le2\sqrt{KT}+10K\log T+16\quad\text{(reduced variance)}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
