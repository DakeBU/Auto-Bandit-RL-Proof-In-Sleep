    algorithm · ordered flow

    Known-reward Hoeffding UCBVI-CH

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Count strict prefixes

      Before episode k, aggregate visits and next-state transitions from episodes strictly earlier than k.

    2. 02
      Estimate transitions

      Normalize transition counts at each state-action pair, with an explicit zero-count convention.

    3. 03
      Plan backward optimistically

      Add a Hoeffding bonus, clip against the horizon and previous Q table, and run backward dynamic programming.

    4. 04
      Execute the greedy policy

      Generate the next episode with the measurable greedy policy from that recurrent table.

    5. 05
      Assemble regret

      Combine confidence and optimism with charge, Bellman-innovation, and low-count bounds on the same process.

    Worked example · follow one decision

    One optimistic Bellman backup in a tiny MDP

    Consider stage h = 1 of a two-stage episode at state s. Two actions have empirical next-stage values 0.5 and 0.2; the second action has been visited only once, so its Hoeffding bonus is much larger.

    1. 01
      Read only earlier episodes

      Strict-prefix counting prevents the current episode from leaking into its own policy. The rarely visited action carries less transition evidence.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Before episode k, the safe action has ten visits and the new action has one visit at this state and stage.\[N_k(1,s,a_{\mathrm{safe}})=10,\qquad N_k(1,s,a_{\mathrm{new}})=1.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Add the transition bonuses

      With zero immediate reward and nonbinding clips, the optimistic backup can prefer the uncertain action even though its empirical next value is lower.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The safe action's optimistic value is 0.6, while the under-sampled action's optimistic value is 0.8.\[Q_k(s,a_{\mathrm{safe}})=0.5+0.1=0.6,\qquad Q_k(s,a_{\mathrm{new}})=0.2+0.6=0.8.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Execute, then narrow the next episode

      The greedy policy tries the new action. If later data leave its empirical value near 0.2 while its bonus falls to 0.2, its score drops below the safe action's 0.6.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Episode k chooses the new action; after its bonus falls to 0.2, its illustrative score becomes 0.4 and no longer beats 0.6.\[\pi_{k,1}(s)=a_{\mathrm{new}},\qquad 0.2+0.2=0.4<0.6.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. UCBVI performs UCB inside backward dynamic programming. Counts control local transition uncertainty, while the proof must propagate optimism and charge those bonuses along the same generated episodic trajectory.

    Scope. The values and bonuses are illustrative and keep clipping inactive for readability. They are not a simulation, do not specify a full transition kernel, and do not claim the Bernstein or unknown-reward variants.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    How local transition confidence becomes episodic regret

    UCBVI repeats the optimism pattern inside dynamic programming. Concentration is local to state-action-stage counts, while the final regret statement is global across a generated sequence of episodes.

    1. 01
      Control empirical transitions

      For every visited state-action-stage tuple, a confidence event bounds the error of applying the empirical transition kernel to a bounded next-value function.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The empirical-transition prediction error for the next value is no larger than its exploration bonus.\[|(\widehat P_k-P)V_{k,h+1}(s,a)|\le b_{k,h}(s,a).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Propagate optimism backward

      Backward induction shows that adding the bonus and clipping at the remaining horizon keeps the recurrent Q table above the optimal value on the good event.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: On the confidence event, the optimistic episode-k Q value dominates the optimal Q value at every stage.\[Q_{k,h}(s,a)\ge Q_h^\star(s,a)\quad\text{on the confidence event}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Decompose one episode

      Optimism turns policy-value regret into a sum of bonuses, transition-estimation errors, and a martingale innovation along the executed trajectory.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The regret of one episode is bounded by the sum of its confidence bonuses, transition errors, and martingale innovations.\[V_1^\star-V_1^{\pi_k}\le\sum_{h=1}^{H}(\text{bonus}_{k,h}+\text{transition error}_{k,h}+\text{innovation}_{k,h}).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Charge visits on the same process

      Strict-prefix counts control how often large bonuses occur; concentration controls the innovation sum, producing the frozen high-probability CH bound.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The cumulative regret is bounded by the explicit 20 times square-root term plus the 250 times lower-order term.\[Regret(K)\le20H^{3/2}L\sqrt{SAK}+250H^2S^2AL^2.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Reading boundary. This bridge is for the known-reward Hoeffding UCBVI-CH route. It does not silently import the Bernstein variant, unknown-reward extensions, or a different trajectory process.

    Source theorem · faithful restatement

    Azar–Osband–Munos, Theorem 1 (UCBVI-CH)

    Original at paper p. 4 ↗

    The UCBVI-CH analysis couples empirical-transition concentration, backward optimism, cumulative bonuses, and a martingale innovation term.

    Model
    An episodic finite MDP with S states, A actions, horizon H, K episodes, and total time T = KH.
    Assumptions
    State and action sets are finite; rewards are known, deterministic, and in [0,1]; transitions are stationary and unknown; the paper's MDP regularity assumption holds.
    Algorithm parameters
    S, A, H, K, confidence δ, and L = log(5HSAT/δ).
    Regret notion
    Cumulative policy-value regret across the first K episodes.
    Guarantee
    With probability at least 1-δ, regret is bounded by the displayed 20/250 UCBVI-CH expression.
