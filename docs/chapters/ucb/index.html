    Reader checkpoint

    Notation you will meet

    N_a(t)
    the number of samples collected from arm a before round t
    μ̂_a(t)
    arm a's empirical mean after those samples
    U_a(t)
    the empirical mean plus a confidence bonus; UCB selects a maximal score
    algorithm · ordered flow

    Upper Confidence Bound policy

    Read top to bottom: each step supplies the state or proof fact used by the next one.

    1. 01
      Initialize

      Pull every arm so that each empirical mean and count is defined.

    2. 02
      Score every arm

      Add a confidence bonus to the arm's empirical mean; less data means a wider bonus.

    3. 03
      Choose optimistically

      Pull an arm with maximal upper-confidence score.

    4. 04
      Update and repeat

      Update that arm's count and empirical mean, then recompute scores next round.

    5. 05
      Read the proof

      On the good event, choosing a suboptimal arm forces its count to still be small; sum gap times count to obtain regret.

    Algorithm · explicit conventions

    Textbook Algorithm 3: UCB(δ)

    This compact form fixes the horizon convention, confidence index, maximizing rule, and update order used in the textbook route.

    UCB score after initialization. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: At round t, score arm a by its empirical mean before t plus square root of 2 log(1/delta) divided by its previous pull count; choose the least-encoded maximizing arm.\[U_a(t)=\widehat\mu_a(t-1)+\sqrt{\frac{2\log(1/\delta)}{N_a(t-1)}},\qquad A_t=\min\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{a\in[k]}U_a(t).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    input: k arms, horizon n, confidence δ = 1/n²
for t = 1,…,k: pull arm t; observe X_t; update N_t and μ̂_t
for t = k+1,…,n:
    compute every U_a(t) from rewards observed before round t
    choose the least-encoded arm in argmax_a U_a(t)
    observe its reward; update only that arm's count and empirical mean

    Source/Lean boundary. The textbook also describes an unpulled arm as having infinite index; the explicit round-robin prefix realizes the same initialization requirement. BanditRLlib's canonical generated route uses round-robin initialization and a least-encoded maximizer, but its native score is parameterized as √(2c log(t+1)/N_a(t)); the source theorem card and exact Lean declarations remain authoritative for constants and indexing.

    Worked example · follow one decision

    Why UCB may deliberately choose the arm with the lower empirical mean

    Take a two-arm UCB(δ) decision near the end of a horizon n = 20, so δ = 1/n² = 1/400. Before round 20, arm 1 has been sampled 15 times and arm 2 only 4 times.

    1. 01
      Start from the observed history

      Arm 1 currently looks better, but arm 2 is much less explored.

      Worked example step 1. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Arm 1 has count 15 and empirical mean 0.60; arm 2 has count 4 and empirical mean 0.30.\[N_1=15,\quad \widehat\mu_1=0.60,\qquad N_2=4,\quad \widehat\mu_2=0.30.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Add uncertainty, not just reward

      With 2 log(400) approximately 11.98, the under-sampled arm receives the wider confidence bonus.

      Worked example step 2. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The two upper-confidence scores are approximately 1.49 for arm 1 and 2.03 for arm 2.\[U_1\approx0.60+\sqrt{11.98/15}=1.49,\qquad U_2\approx0.30+\sqrt{11.98/4}=2.03.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Choose optimism and shrink it with data

      UCB selects arm 2 even though its empirical mean is lower. Each additional pull increases N₂, narrows its bonus, and makes persistent suboptimal selection harder to justify.

      Worked example step 3. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: At round 20 the policy chooses arm 2 because its upper-confidence score is larger; its bonus decreases as its sample count increases.\[A_{20}=2\quad\text{because}\quad U_2>U_1;\qquad \sqrt{2\log(1/\delta)/N_2}\downarrow\text{ as }N_2\uparrow.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Takeaway. UCB is not greedily choosing the largest empirical mean. It chooses the largest plausible mean, so uncertainty creates exploration and the count-dependent radius eventually limits repeated pulls of a genuinely suboptimal arm.

    Scope. The numbers illustrate one UCB(δ) decision; they are not a simulated regret result or a new Lean theorem. The source card and compiled declarations below control the exact assumptions and constants.

    Proof bridge · source to Lean

    Why an optimistic choice becomes a regret bound

    The source proof and the local Lean route use the same four conceptual joints. Read this bridge before comparing constants or implementation details.

    1. 01
      Control every empirical mean

      Work on a confidence event where each empirical mean stays within its declared radius of the true arm mean.

      Step 1 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: For every relevant arm and round, the empirical mean error is at most its confidence radius.\[|\widehat\mu_a(t)-\mu_a|\le r_a(t).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    2. 02
      Use optimism at the selected arm

      If a suboptimal arm is chosen on that event, its upper-confidence score must still compete with the optimal arm's mean.

      Step 2 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Selecting a suboptimal arm on the confidence event forces its gap to be no larger than twice its current radius.\[A_t=a\ne a^\star\Longrightarrow \Delta_a\le 2r_a(t).\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    3. 03
      Turn the radius into a count threshold

      Because the radius shrinks with the number of samples, the preceding inequality can hold only while the arm has been pulled comparatively few times.

      Step 3 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The pull count of a suboptimal arm is controlled by a logarithmic term divided by the squared arm gap.\[N_a(T)\lesssim \frac{\log(T/\delta_a)}{\Delta_a^2}.\]Swipe to read the full formula →
    4. 04
      Charge every pull by its gap

      Apply the pathwise gap-sum identity, then account explicitly for the confidence-event failure probability under the generated trajectory law.

      Step 4 equation. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: Expected pseudo-regret is at most the sum of each positive gap times its pull-count bound plus its explicit failure-event charge.\[\mathbb E\bar R_T\le\sum_{a:\Delta_a>0}\Delta_a\bigl(B_a(T)+T\delta_a\bigr).\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    Source theorem · faithful restatement

    Theorem 7.2 (finite-time UCB regret)

    Original at online p. 108 ↗

    Confidence validity implies a suboptimal arm can be selected only while its sample count is small enough to keep its bonus competitive.

    Model
    A finite stochastic k-armed bandit in the textbook's 1-sub-Gaussian environment class.
    Assumptions
    Each arm has a fixed mean and 1-sub-Gaussian noise; the chapter's canonical independent reward-table model supplies the samples used by the proof.
    Algorithm parameters
    Algorithm 3, UCB(δ), at horizon n with δ = 1/n²; an unpulled arm has infinite index, otherwise the index is its empirical mean plus √(2 log(1/δ) / count).
    Regret notion
    The textbook expected regret Rₙ over n rounds.
    Guarantee
    A worst-case finite-time upper bound 8√(nk log n) plus three times the sum of arm gaps.
    Source mathematical statement. Formula renderer unavailable; readable fallback: The textbook UCB policy has a finite-time regret bound with a leading square-root term in horizon, arm count, and log horizon.\[R_n\le 8\sqrt{nk\log n}+3\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_i.\]Swipe to read the full formula →

    BanditRLlib relationship. BanditRLlib's ordinary-UCB route uses an explicit generated trajectory and confidence schedule. Its exact hypotheses, constants, gap-sum form, and Tδ failure term are therefore not presented as a line-for-line formalization of Theorem 7.2; KL-UCB remains a separate extension.
