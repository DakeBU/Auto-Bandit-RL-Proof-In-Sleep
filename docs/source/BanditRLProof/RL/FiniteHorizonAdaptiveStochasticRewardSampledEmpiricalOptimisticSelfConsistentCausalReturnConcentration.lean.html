5 # Sampled-return concentration on the heterogeneous causal source
6 
7 This module transports two adaptive sampled-return concentration processes to
8 the dependent source whose coordinate `n` contains `episodes n` complete
9 stochastic episodes.  The supporting process uses the initial policy and
10 `episodes 0` at coordinate zero, then the prefix-selected policy and
11 `episodes (n + 1)` at coordinate `n + 1`; each batch is centered at its own
12 sampled initial-state value.  The regret-facing global process is zero at
13 coordinate zero and globally centers successor coordinates `1..rounds` by the
14 initial-law expected value of the selected policy.  Its measurable-selector
15 boundary is packaged by `GlobalReturnMeasurability`.
16 
17 The proof route maps each exact selected iid batch fiber to its sampled-return
18 deviation, identifies the dynamic conditional law through the prefix/next
19 `compProd` theorem, obtains the corresponding conditional sub-Gaussian MGF,
20 and applies the existing strongly-adapted finite-sum tail theorem.  Each total
21 variance proxy is the genuine heterogeneous sum of its coordinate proxies;
22 the global proxy also retains sampled-initial-state value fluctuation.
23 
24 Regularity is finite measurable nonempty State/Action with measurable
25 singletons, a probability initial law, Standard Borel State/Action for regular
26 conditional laws, a uniform selected-reward sub-Gaussian law, and a deterministic
27 bound on stored mean rewards.  The generic tail keeps strict positivity of the
28 summed proxy explicit.
29 
30 Failure policy: this proves sampled-return concentration for the new
31 round-varying latest-batch causal algorithm.  It does not transport empirical
32 model confidence, optimism, regret, or any rate from the old constant-parameter
33 window laws.  It is fixed-`rounds`, not uniform-in-time, pathwise,
34 almost-sure, anytime, minimax, or complete UCB-VI control.
35 -/
