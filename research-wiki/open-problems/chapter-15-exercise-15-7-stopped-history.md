# Chapter 15 Exercise 15.7 stopped-history information bridge

## Exact target

For the canonical finite-arm bandit law generated by one common randomized
nonanticipating policy, a bounded stopping time `tau`, and an `F_tau`-measurable
random element `X`, prove the source-direction inequality

`D(P_nu^X,P_nu'^X) <= sum_i E_nu[T_i(tau)] D(P_i,P_i')`.

This is Lattimore--Szepesvari, *Bandit Algorithms* (2020), Exercise 15.7. The
source hint routes through Exercises 14.10 and 14.9.

## Current compiled leaf

- `LowerBounds.klDiv_map_le` proves generic KL data processing under an
  arbitrary measurable map of finite measures, including the infinite source-
  KL branch.
- `LowerBounds.klDiv_observedBanditHistory_le_expectedPulls_sum` combines that
  leaf with Lemma 15.1 for a deterministic complete history.

These declarations are dependencies, not the stopping-time theorem.

## Smallest missing route

1. Define or expose a measurable stopped-history object for a bounded stopping
   time on the canonical history space.
2. Prove that its KL is bounded by the first-law arm information accumulated
   only through `tau`; a finite stopping-fibre decomposition is the preferred
   first route.
3. Factor any `F_tau`-measurable `X` through that stopped history, or state the
   exact measurable factorization contract explicitly, then apply
   `klDiv_map_le`.

## Regularity and failure fence

Keep the same randomized policy under both environments, the `nu -> nu'` KL
direction, first-law expected pull counts, bounded positive stopping time, and
countably generated reward/observation requirements explicit. Do not replace
the stopped count by a deterministic terminal-horizon count, invoke optional
stopping without a martingale/integrability contract, or promote the compiled
data-processing leaf to Exercise 15.7.
