# Actual FTL ordinary-limit semantics

For one real observation stream, squared-loss FTL starts at 1/2 and thereafter predicts the mean of observations strictly before the current time. Its actual cumulative loss is at least the loss at the horizon empirical mean. Signed regret against any fixed real comparator is that empirical-mean gap minus the horizon times the squared distance from the comparator to the empirical mean. Both algebraic results include horizon zero and allow arbitrary real streams; they do not claim unit-game feasibility for unbounded observations.

For one infinite stream with every observation in [0,1], normalized interval-best regret tends to zero. Consequently normalized regret against a fixed real comparator tends to a real a exactly when its squared distance from the horizon empirical mean tends to -a. This equivalence does not assert that either limit exists. If empirical-mean convergence to m is explicitly assumed, every real comparator has normalized regret limit -(u-m)^2. Restriction to unit comparators supplies the literal nonpositive ordinary-limit predicate. The mean m is an analysis parameter, never a learner input.
