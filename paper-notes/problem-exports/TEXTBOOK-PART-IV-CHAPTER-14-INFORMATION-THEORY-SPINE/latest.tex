\paragraph{Proof map.}
A local binary prefix code assigns distinct nonempty bit strings to source
symbols and forbids one codeword from prefixing a distinct one. Induction on
message lists proves that its range is uniquely decodable, so Mathlib's
Kraft--McMillan theorem gives
\[
  \sum_{w\in C}2^{-|w|}\le 1.
\]
For finite support, the local natural and base-two entropies satisfy
\[
 H(P)=\sum_x p_x\log(p_x^{-1}),\qquad
 H_2(P)=\frac{H(P)}{\log 2},
\]
and $H(P)\ge0$ for masses in $[0,1]$. Expected code length is
$\sum_x p_x|c(x)|$. These facts do not imply Huffman optimality.

For probability measures on a finite alphabet with measurable singletons,
put $p_x=P(\{x\})$ and $q_x=Q(\{x\})$. Finite atomic additivity gives
$P\ll Q$ if and only if $q_x=0$ implies $p_x=0$ at every symbol.
Evaluating the with-density identity on each singleton yields
$(dP/dQ)(x)q_x=p_x$. Finite-domain integrability and expansion of the
log-likelihood integral therefore give Eq.~(14.4):
\[
D(P\Vert Q)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_x p_x\log(p_x/q_x),&\operatorname{supp}P\subseteq\operatorname{supp}Q,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Zero source terms are zero; a positive source atom absent from $Q$ forces
the infinite branch. The local declarations are
\texttt{absolutelyContinuous\_iff\_atom\_support},
\texttt{rnDeriv\_mul\_atom}, \texttt{rnDeriv\_atom\_eq\_div},
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_eq\_top\_of\_atom\_support\_mismatch},
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_finite\_klFun},
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_finite\_sum\_log},
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_finite\_eq\_if}, and
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_finite\_eq\_top\_iff}.
The convex-integrand identity also applies to finite measures.
No strict positivity or extra log-integrability premise is assumed in the
probability formula.

For Eq.~(14.5), let $D_{\mathrm{fin}}(P,Q)$ be the supremum of the
divergences of $P\circ f^{-1}$ and $Q\circ f^{-1}$ over all measurable
$f:\Omega\to\operatorname{Fin}(n)$ and all $n$. Fiberwise Jensen and integral
decomposition give $D_{\mathrm{fin}}(P,Q)\le D(P\Vert Q)$. In the singular
case, a measurable $Q$-null set of positive $P$ mass supplies a binary
observation with infinite divergence. Under $P\ll Q$, simple approximants
generate a filtration resolving the RN density. Upward conditional-expectation
convergence, continuity of the convex KL integrand, and Fatou recover the
original divergence as the supremum of the trimmed divergences. Each stage's
joint simple-approximant values have finite range and can be encoded in
$\operatorname{Fin}(n)$. The observation/comap identity and trim monotonicity
therefore give the reverse bound, proving
\[
 D_{\mathrm{fin}}(P,Q)=D(P\Vert Q).
\]
The terminal is \texttt{finitePartitionRelativeEntropy\_eq\_relativeEntropy}.
Its principal bridges are \texttt{relativeEntropy\_finite\_map\_le},
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_eq\_iSup\_densityApproximation\_trim},
\texttt{exists\_fin\_observation\_densityApproximation},
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_map\_eq\_trim\_of\_\allowbreak absolutelyContinuous}, and
\texttt{relativeEntropy\_trim\_mono}; the synchronized Markdown declaration
list names every helper. The equality holds for finite measures on arbitrary
measurable spaces, without finite-KL, AC, or countable-generation premises.
The probability specialization and existing RN branch adapters retain the
singular and nonintegrable infinity cases in Theorem~14.1.

For finite measures on $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$,
the infinite-KL branch is immediate. In the finite branch, the RN derivative
after trimming equals the conditional expectation of the original density.
Conditional Jensen for the convex KL integrand and integral transport yield
\[
 D(P|_{\mathcal G}\Vert Q|_{\mathcal G})\le D(P\Vert Q).
\]
This is the full sub-$\sigma$-algebra form of Exercise 14.10.

Finally, event data processing, the endpoint-complete binary affinity bound,
and antitonicity of the testing scale prove
\[
 \frac12\exp\{-D(P\Vert Q)\}\le P(A)+Q(A^{\mathrm c}),
\]
with the left side defined as zero at infinite KL. The direction is $P$ to
$Q$, and the complement is evaluated under $Q$.
